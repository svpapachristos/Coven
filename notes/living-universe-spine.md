# The Living Universe: the spine of the Unmaking
Written Oct 10, 2026. This is the north star for the Hexweaver's ultimate. Every phase, tweak and polish pass should serve it. The bar: **Deadlock-level polish**. The feeling: **childlike awe**, the power of an Infinity Stone.

## The thesis
The Unmaking reveals that she doesn't intend to obtain godhood: she always had the power. Each cast tears her world open and turns her enemies into the raw matter of a universe that **she owns, tends and grows**: stardust, nebulae, stars, supernovae, black holes, and eventually a supermassive black hole at the heart of her own galaxy. It is a living sandbox in which the player is god, and a whole game inside a single ability. Everything in it follows **real astrophysics, kept simple**: real rules that are easy to code and show on screen, never a simulation to maintain.

## 1. The world: one sky per run, and one budget of mass
Her sky persists for the whole run and only exists on screen during the ult. Everything in it is **mass** in one of four forms:
- **Gas:** the haze. Wisps colored by what made them.
- **Stars:** color and size from mass, from red dwarf to blue giant.
- **Remnants:** what dead stars leave behind (white dwarfs, neutron stars and pulsars, supernova remnant filaments).
- **Black holes:** at most 3 per run. Each one only grows.
Mass only moves between forms. Enemies add it, black holes remove it. That is what keeps the sky bounded over a run while still building toward a supermassive.

## 2. The cast: what one ult looks like (LOUD, quiet, LOUD)
1. **LOUD, the entrance.** The glitch, the stillness, the sigil, the crack, the impact frame, the BOOM, and her planet splitting into slabs over a chasm.
2. **quiet, the snap.** A held breath: just her and her full-color horde in her universe. A quiet crumble ripples out. Every body breaks into its own pixels, which drift like ash, catch, and swirl around her into her sky.
3. **The catch.** Dust glows into gas, leaves haze behind, and condenses into protostars. Each star catches silently when its last mote lands. Then the finale ring.
4. **LOUD, live events (if one is due).** At most one big event per cast: a supernova or a collapse into a black hole, played in full on screen.
5. **The return.** The world slams back (eventually rebuilt as the entrance in reverse).

## 3. Between casts: time passes in her universe (the time gate). THE CAST IS THE PEEK
**Decided Oct 10, 8:50pm** (after Steps 1.3 and 1.4 showed that thirty births in three seconds reads as glittery soup): the cast is the moment she *looks into* her universe, so each cast does two jobs:
1. **Catch up (first seconds of the time stop):** what happened while she fought plays out fast, on screen. Old haze has gathered into a **few** cocoons, threads crawl in slowly, and stars switch on **one or a few at a time**, each lighting its own little pocket of gas. Aged stars have shifted. That is where "stardust coalescing into the genesis of fusion" lives: a few places at once, with room to notice each one.
2. **Feed (the snap):** this cast's enemies become fresh dust and haze, food for the next peek. Stars from this cast's own clumps ignite directly, with no birth staging.
The perpetual sandbox layers on top (so it feels alive, not scripted): haze slowly gathering, stars aging, gentle orbits and drift, deaths, holes feeding. Each peek in a 15-minute slice should show something new: (1) nebula and first stars, (2) births from old haze, (3) first planetary nebula, (4) supernova and black hole.
Her sky keeps living while she's away. When the sky opens on the next cast, cosmic time catches up by an amount tied to the time since the last cast:
- Stars age by mass.
- Dense gas collapses into new stars, born from her old kills with no enemy needed.
- Gentle deaths happen off screen: planetary nebulae and white dwarfs.
- **The reveal:** the sky she returns to has visibly changed. A ring nebula sits where a yellow star used to be, and there are new stars in last cast's haze.
Rule: **quiet changes happen between casts, big events happen on screen.** Every cast shows something new.

## 4. The matter cycle: the real life of a star, in her hands
enemy → **dust** (each mote the color of the pixel it came off) → **gas** (the glow of what it's made of) → **haze** (persistent: her nebula, a record of her kills) → **protostar** → **star**
- Gas colors by hue: hydrogen red-pink, oxygen teal, sulphur gold, reflection blue-violet, her violet. Dark pixels become soot (dark dust lanes).
- A star's color and brightness come from its mass: a lone enemy makes a red dwarf, a clump a yellow-white sun, a great crowd a blue giant.
- **How a star dies depends on its mass:**
  - **Red dwarf:** endures. The steady background of her sky.
  - **Sun-like (yellow-white):** a **planetary nebula** (a glowing ring like the Ring or Helix, "the Eye of God") plus a white dwarf.
  - **Blue giant:** a **supernova**, leaving a **remnant nebula** (filaments in oxygen teal and sulphur / iron gold: elements forged in the star) plus a **neutron star / pulsar**.
  - **The heaviest:** core collapse into a **black hole** (the five-beat spectacle: unstable, it pulls, implosion, BOOM, reveal).
- **Deaths return less than they took** (roughly 70-85% goes back to gas, and the remnant keeps the rest). Remnant nebulae give her sky colors that kills alone can't make. New stars form from them, and holes feed on them.

## 5. Black holes: the climax of a run
- At most 3 per run, spread across her sky (spacing rules), and only once she has ascended enough.
- Each one is born through the full spectacle: a blue giant swells and pulses, time drags, it drinks nearby stars and haze in spiral streams, implodes, a near-black beat, the impact frame, BOOM, the supernova shell, and the hole revealed at the heart of its remnant.
- **The newborn's temperature journey:** an ember ring (EHT) → magenta → white-hot inner edge, with the hairline photon ring snapping in last.
- **The look** (Gargantua plus EHT plus our palette): the disk band in front, the far side lensed over the top and under the bottom, luminous bloom, the Doppler bright side drifting with the spin, background stars lensed into arcs. Three angles across the three holes (edge-on, a tilted "eye", near face-on).
- **Holes feed and grow.** Their disks are woven from the stars and haze they swallow (spaghettified streams), so a hole's history is visible.

## 6. Endless mode: the galaxy
- The three holes slowly drift toward each other and inspiral. Ripples (gravitational waves) run through the whole sky, then they merge in the biggest event in the game.
- The **supermassive black hole** settles at the heart of her sky, a screen-dominating Gargantua. Over time her stars, haze and remnants organise into **orbits and spiral arms** around it: **her universe becomes a galaxy.**
- It stays alive forever. New kills keep adding mass, stars keep being born and dying, and the supermassive keeps feeding. Bounded, but never static.

- **The slice tuning must show the whole story in a normal 15-minute run:** births from haze, at least one planetary nebula, a supernova, at least one black hole. Endless gets all three holes, the merger and the galaxy.

## 11. State of the sandbox (Oct 10, 10:15pm): where we are, and the terrarium loop
**Built and working:**
- **The snap:** the horde crumbles into pixel-colored dust, which becomes soft gas colored by element (hydrogen pink, oxygen teal, sulphur gold, violet, soot). This cast's clumps ignite as stars, colored by mass.
- **The nebula persists:** `wisp_keep` about 0.475; the fresh haze brightens to take over from the fading dust. The horde's shape is written into the sky.
- **The peek (cast = the window):** births from old haze (Bok globule, Keplerian infall with comet trails, protostar, Herbig-Haro jets, catch plus light front, bubble). Up to 4 per cast, 1 per `HEX_BIRTH_EVERY` seconds away.
- **Aging:** sun-like stars redden and swell into red giants, then die during a peek as a lumpy planetary nebula ring plus a white dwarf; the ring becomes haze. Debug keys: B (birth at the mouse), N (+60s cosmic time).

**The terrarium loop (the design rule from here on):** mass flows in a circle, and **every source needs a sink**, or the sky clogs and whites out.
- **Grow:** kills add gas (source).
- **Condense:** dense gas collapses into stars (sink for gas). It must actually *use up* the gas: wisps consumed, not just dimmed.
- **Wither:** stars age; light ones endure, mid ones puff rings, heavy ones go supernova (later).
- **Recycle:** deaths return 70-85% as gas, enriched (rings: H plus O; supernovae: O plus S/Fe gold).
- **Lock away:** white dwarfs, neutron stars and black holes keep the rest. Black holes also feed on gas and stars (the final sink).
- **Diffuse:** old gas slowly spreads and thins over many casts unless something replenishes it.
Result: an equilibrium that shifts with how she plays. Lots of kills give a bright, busy nursery; few give a quiet, old sky of red dwarfs, rings and remnants.

**Foundation problem seen after a few casts:** haze stacks additively and gets very bright in dense patches. Fix by making dense gas the thing that collapses (density-driven births that consume it), by having births scale with total gas, by slow diffusion, and by a soft brightness ceiling for the haze.
